Laplace Transforms

Carry initial conditions into an algebraic equation in s and recover the time response.

Exam 1 study guide

Handwritten notes (PDF) for Laplace Transforms Lecture PDF for Laplace Transforms

Opening questions

  • How does the cart’s initial speed enter the Laplace transform of its force balance?

  • What can you check in a recovered time response before plotting it?

Transform the equation and the initial conditions

The unilateral Laplace transform weights \(x(t)\) by \(e^{-st}\) and integrates from \(0\) to \(\infty\). For derivatives, the initial conditions enter as

\[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}\{\dot{x}\} &= sX-x(0),\\ \mathcal{L}\{\ddot{x}\} &= s^2X-sx(0)-\dot{x}(0). \end{aligned}\]

These initial-condition terms distinguish the full response from a transfer-function calculation.

Useful pairs include \(\mathcal{L}\{1\}=1/s\), \(\mathcal{L}\{e^{-at}\}=1/(s+a)\), and \(\mathcal{L}\{\sin(\omega t)\}=\omega/(s^2+\omega^2)\). A delay of \(T\) with a causal switch multiplies the transform by \(e^{-sT}\).

Solve and invert

Worked example · Recover the time response

For \(\dot{x}+2x=4\) and \(x(0)=1\), the transform gives \((s+2)X=4/s+1\). Decompose \(X=2/s-1/(s+2)\) and invert to get \(x(t)=2-e^{-2t}\). The initial value and equilibrium verify the signs.

Distinct poles give sums of exponentials. A repeated factor \(1/(s+a)^2\) gives \(te^{-at}\). For a conjugate pair, complete the square and split the numerator so it matches shifted sine and cosine transforms.

Work through a Laplace solution

Given. A 1 kg mass slides on a frictionless surface. The damper coefficient is 3 N·s/m, the applied step force is 6 N to the right, and the initial velocity is 1 m/s to the right.

Predict. Which term carries the initial value into the transformed equation?

Try. Use the free-body diagram to write the velocity equation. Select the transform of the derivative, then reveal each step when you have tried it yourself.

If V(s) is the transform of velocity, what is the transform of its derivative?

Step 1 · Write the force balance

Positive force acts to the right and the damper opposes velocity.

Applied force minus damping force equals mass times acceleration, so v′ + 3v = 6 with v(0) = 1.
Step 2 · Transform the ODE

Subtract the initial value from the derivative term and collect the terms in V.

(s + 3)V = 6/s + 1.
Step 3 · Split into simple fractions

Write the transformed response as a steady contribution and a decaying contribution.

V = 2/s − 1/(s + 3).
Step 4 · Invert and check

At time zero the response is 1. As time increases, it approaches 2.

v(t) = 2 − exp(−3t) m/s.

Explain. Check that the solution has the given initial value and approaches the equilibrium implied by the original equation.

Watch the two forces come into balance

Predict. What happens to the net force as the cart speeds up?

Reference case

At release, the 6 N applied force opposes a 3 N damper force, leaving 3 N net. At steady speed, both force magnitudes approach 6 N.

Key takeaways

  • Carry initial conditions into the unilateral transform when transforming derivatives.

  • Solve for the transformed variable, then use partial fractions or known transform pairs to recover the response.

  • Check that the solution starts at the given value and, for a stable constant-input case, approaches the equilibrium.
