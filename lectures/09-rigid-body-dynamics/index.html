Rigid-Body Dynamics

Opening questions

  • As a pinned rod swings, how does the moment arm of its weight change?

  • How can the rod be moving fastest when its angular acceleration is zero?

Start with a free-body diagram

Choose a system boundary and positive directions. Account for applied forces, weight, reactions, and friction. For planar rigid-body motion, apply \(\sum\mathbf{F}=m\mathbf{a}_G\) to the center of mass and \(\sum M_G=I_G\alpha\) about it. For rotation about a fixed axis, the moment of inertia about that axis can be found with \(I_O=I_G+md^2\).

Work through a moment balance

Given. A uniform 2 kg rod is 1.2 m long and pinned at one end. Release it from rest at 30° from the downward vertical. Its center of mass is 0.60 m from the pin.

Predict. Where does the weight of a uniform rod act when you take moments about its pin?

Try. Identify the weight’s line of action and its perpendicular distance from the pin. The pin reactions pass through the pivot, so they make no moment about it. Then work through the balance.

What is the perpendicular lever arm of the weight?

Step 1 · Write the moment of gravity

Define positive angle away from the downward vertical in the direction of the initial displacement. Gravity acts to reduce that positive angle.

Moment about the pin = −mg(L/2)sin θ.
Step 2 · Use the inertia about the pin

The moment of inertia of the uniform rod about its pinned end is 0.96 kg·m².

I about the pin = mL²/3 = 0.96 kg·m².
Step 3 · Find the initial acceleration

The negative sign shows that the acceleration tends to bring the rod back toward the downward vertical.

Initial angular acceleration ≈ −6.13 rad/s².

Explain. How would the angular acceleration change if the rod were released on the opposite side of the vertical?

Watch gravity turn the pinned rod

Predict. Where in the swing will the magnitude of angular acceleration be greatest?

Try. Release the 1.2 m uniform rod from rest. Scrub through its first crossing and watch the weight’s moment arm change sign.

Explain. Explain how the angular acceleration can be zero at the instant the rod swings fastest.

Reference case

At 30° the moment arm is 0.30 m and the angular acceleration is approximately −6.13 rad/s². At the downward crossing the moment arm and acceleration are zero, and the rod has its highest speed.

Angular acceleration is −3g sin θ divided by twice the rod length. The model neglects pivot friction.

Energy and friction

For a particle or rigid body, work changes kinetic energy. With conservative forces represented by potential energy, work by the remaining forces changes the sum of kinetic and potential energy. Static friction supplies the force required to avoid slip until its limiting magnitude is reached. During sliding, kinetic friction opposes the relative velocity.

Pendulum example

For a point mass on a massless link, moment balance about the pivot gives \(m\ell^2\ddot{\theta}=-mg\ell\sin\theta\). Near the downward equilibrium, \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\) for \(\theta\) in radians, so \(\ddot{\theta}+(g/\ell)\theta\approx0\). The small-angle frequency \(\sqrt{g/\ell}\) follows from the linearized model.

Compare the small- and large-angle motion of a lightly damped point pendulum in Phase Portraits and Stability.

Skill check · Physical modeling

A uniform rod hangs from a pin at one end. Where does its weight act?

Worked answer

A uniform rod’s center of mass lies halfway along its length.

Key takeaways

  • Start with a free-body diagram. Mark the positive directions, the point for taking moments, and the lines of action.

  • The pin reactions pass through the pivot and contribute zero moment about it. Gravity’s moment is the weight times its signed perpendicular lever arm.

  • At the downward crossing of the frictionless rod, the gravity moment vanishes and the angular speed reaches its maximum.
