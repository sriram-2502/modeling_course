Modeling and Analysis of Dynamic Systems Sriram Krishnamoorthy

Modeling and Analysis of Dynamic Systems

Lectures

Read the lectures by topic, with figures, worked examples, and simulations on the lecture pages.

Dynamic response and modeling foundations

  1. Lecture 01

    Course Overview and Math Recap

    Frame a modeling problem by choosing what to predict, what can change, and what data can test the prediction.

  2. Lecture 02

    Math Review

    Review integration, vector operations, and complex numbers used in dynamic modeling.

  3. Lecture 03

    First-Order Systems

    Use equilibria, time constants, phase lines, and linearization to predict first-order motion.

  4. Lecture 04

    Second-Order Systems

    Connect characteristic roots to the free response of a mass–spring–damper system.

  5. Lecture 05

    Laplace Transforms

    Carry initial conditions into an algebraic equation in s and recover the time response.

  6. Lecture 06

    Convolution and System Response

    Find the output of a linear time-invariant model by accumulating shifted impulse responses.

  7. Lecture 07

    Transfer Functions and Stability

    Derive an input-to-output transfer function and use its poles to interpret the response.

  8. Lecture 08

    Exam 1 Review

    Practice moving between ODEs, equilibria, Laplace transforms, and time responses.

Mechanical systems and stability

  1. Lecture 09

    Rigid-Body Dynamics

    Apply force, moment, and energy balances to derive equations of motion.

  2. Lecture 10

    Drive Kinematics

    Model pulleys, belts, and gear pairs with kinematic constraints and torque balances.

  3. Lecture 11

    Springs and State Space

    Build spring models and express a second-order ODE as a first-order state model.

  4. Lecture 12

    Dampers and State Space

    Use force laws and state equations to distinguish storage, dissipation, and measured outputs.

  5. Lecture 13

    Transfer Functions and Block Diagrams

    Translate force balances into integrator blocks and trace the signals to an output.

  6. Lecture 14

    Coupled Systems and the Quarter-Car Model

    Connect relative spring and damper forces to a two-mass dynamic model.

  7. Lecture 15

    Phase Portraits and Stability

    Relate equilibria, linearization, eigenvalues, and the geometry of trajectories.

  8. Lecture 16

    Exam 2 Review

    Practice converting mechanical models into state equations, block diagrams, and transfer functions.

Electrical, fluid, and thermal systems

  1. Lecture 17

    Electrical Circuits

    Apply Kirchhoff’s laws to derive RC, RL, and RLC dynamics.

  2. Lecture 18

    Electromechanical Coupling

    Predict the force on a current-carrying rod and the voltage across the same rod when it moves.

  3. Lecture 19

    Galvanometers and DC Motors

    Combine current, torque, speed, and back EMF in a coupled electromechanical model.

  4. Lecture 20

    Fluid Systems and Mass Conservation

    Derive liquid-level and mixing models from conservation of mass.

  5. Lecture 21

    Fluid Systems and Electrical Analogies

    Model hydraulic pressure, restrictions, capacitance, and piston forces.

  6. Lecture 22

    Thermal Systems

    Use energy conservation, thermal resistance, and capacitance to predict temperature.

System representations and feedback

  1. Lecture 23

    Connecting System Representations

    Explain how ODEs, state space, transfer functions, and phase portraits describe the same dynamics.

  2. Lecture 24

    Introduction to Feedback Control

    Use a feedback diagram to explain how a controller changes a system’s response.
